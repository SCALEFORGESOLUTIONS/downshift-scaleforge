Speed

From a sentence to something you can publish, in about a minute.

What happens in that minute

Sketchie writes the script on story structure, breaks it into scenes, picks the analogy, draws every element, narrates it, and binds each reveal to the word that triggers it.

And then in seconds

A change re-renders only the scenes it touches. Not a queue, not a revision round, not an email to an animator who is on holiday.

The number that matters

Not the first render — the whole loop. Draft, watch, notice, fix, ship. That is the only clock a buyer actually pays for.

Length is set in 30-second steps, or left automatic. Six minutes is the ceiling, on purpose — slide 7 explains why that is a decision rather than a limit.

The editor is not an upsell

Full scene editor. Every plan. Including the free one.

Change anything in a scene

Heading, narration, labels, icon styling. Move through with Previous and Next. When the fields are not enough, describe the change in plain language instead.

Versions, not overwrites

Every edit appends a version to the same explainer, and you can point the head back at an earlier ready one. Useful the second time a stakeholder changes their mind.

The first edit of each video is free

And editing never consumes one of the two free videos. After that a targeted fix draws a few seconds of minutes rather than a whole new video.

This is architecture rather than a feature. The video is compiled from an addressable document, which is why a correction can touch one scene — and why a variant is a re-render rather than a re-commission.

If you want to automate it

One POST in. MP4 and scene graph out.

Creation is asynchronous — 202 and a queued record, then poll or hold a Server-Sent Events stream. The edit endpoint turns one plain-language instruction into a targeted re-render; revert points the head back. Post a scene graph you already have with a new aspect or language and you get that variant.

Generation endpoints allow 20 requests a minute, everything else shares 200. An MCP server with five tools runs as a local stdio process for Claude Code and Claude Desktop; a hosted connector is on the roadmap.

Where we are behind, today.

Raw first-generation speed

Sketchie's own comparison page concedes a competitor is faster at the first render. The argument is the whole loop, including the fix — and it has to be demonstrated, not asserted.

Single-user

No shared workspace today; shared workspaces are on the roadmap. Do not sell team collaboration.

No proof but the output

No customers, counts, testimonials or press are published, and the company states it launched in July 2026 with no venture funding. The nineteen renders and the open eval repo are the evidence that exists.

Conceding the metric you lose on is the most efficient way to be believed on the two you win.
