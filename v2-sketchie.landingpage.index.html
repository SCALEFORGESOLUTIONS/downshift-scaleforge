One source, many outputs

The expensive part of video is the second version. Here it is the cheap part.

Everywhere else, "can we get a vertical cut, and a Spanish one, and a thirty-second version for the ad" is three more quotes and three more weeks. Sketchie's videos are compiled from a scene graph, so a variant is a re-render of a source you already have rather than a new project.

Three shapes

16:9 for the docs site, 9:16 for vertical feeds, 1:1 for the square slot. Same explainer, laid out for where it is going.

Any length in 30-second steps

A three-minute walkthrough and a thirty-second cut of the same idea. Up to six minutes; longer material becomes a chaptered series.

In your audience's language

Generated natively in-language — script, on-screen labels and narration, not subtitles pasted over an English render. Every narrator speaks every supported language.

Six drawing styles

marker, chalkboard, pencil, blueprint, crayon and clean, plus four fill techniques. The same explanation, dressed for a classroom or a board deck.

Six narrators

Nora, Miles, Dean, Clara, Piper and Wren, previewable before you generate. Nora narrates by default.

Or just fix the one wrong sentence

Type what is wrong in plain language. Only the affected scenes re-render, and every re-render lands as a new version you can revert from.

Editing never uses up one of your free videos and the first edit of each video is free. After that a targeted fix draws a few seconds of minutes rather than a whole new video, and a full alternate cut renders like any other video against your plan minutes.

The editor is not an upsell

The full scene editor is on every plan, including the free one.

Open the scene list, click a scene, and change the heading, the narration, the labels or the icon styling. Move through with Previous and Next. When the fields are not enough, describe the change you want in plain language instead. Save, and it re-renders.

Every edit appends a version to the same explainer rather than replacing it, so nothing is overwritten and you can point back at an earlier cut whenever a stakeholder changes their mind twice.

Not a manifesto, a gate

Fast and cheap only count if the video is good.

Which is the fair objection to everything above. Sketchie's answer is a published spec: eleven design laws taken from the research on learning from video, each carrying its effect size and a link to its primary source, turned into numeric targets the generator is checked against on every render — with its own confidence grade printed next to each one.

The gate runs before the money does

Mechanical checks run on the scene graph at generation time, before anything is spent on voice or rendering: duration ceiling, per-scene band, element count, reveal words that resolve, the post-scene silence, the question-and-recap ending. A graph that fails goes back with its own failures fed into the prompt. That layer is MIT licensed and runnable, which is an unusual thing to be able to check for yourself.

Why six minutes is a ceiling, not a limit

The spec cites Guo et al. (2014, Learning@Scale), an analysis of 6.9 million edX sessions reporting engagement near 1.0 under six minutes, around 0.55 at nine to twelve, and roughly 0.2 past twelve.

That research is about learning from video in general. It is not a measurement of Sketchie's videos, and no such measurement exists. Read the paper · Read the full spec
