How it works

Explaining things is brutal. This is the shortcut.

The doc nobody reads, the recording nobody watches, the deck that puts the room to sleep. Four steps instead.

1

Drop anything in

A prompt, a PDF, a Word doc, Markdown, your product docs. Sketchie reads it and writes a script built on story structure.

2

Watch it draw

Every element draws on stroke by stroke, timed to the exact word of narration it belongs to. The biggest retention lever in the research.

3

Fix what is off

Open the scene list, click a scene, edit the text, visual or narration. Sketchie re-renders that scene only, in seconds.

4

Ship it

Export MP4 in any of three aspect ratios, or pull the scene graph through the API and do whatever you want with it.

Sticky, not viral

Fast and cheap only matter if the video is good. Sketchie's generator enforces rules drawn from published, peer-reviewed research, and every rule carries its evidence grade so you can tell a verified effect from a working assumption.

L1 · Verified

Everything draws on, always

Content built up stroke by stroke beats showing completed scenes, for both retention and transfer. This is the core mechanism, so we never break it.

L5 · Verified

Six minutes is a cliff

Across 6.9 million edX sessions, engagement sits near total under 6 minutes, about half at 9 to 12, and roughly a fifth past 12. We cap at 6:00 and chapter anything longer.

L9 · Verified

Every video ends with a question

A retrieval beat: the question appears, the video pauses, then a one-line recap. Making the learner generate the answer improves learning.

L4 · Verified

Pauses are on purpose

After a scene's final reveal, narration holds silent for at least 0.7 seconds before the cut. Processing time at boundaries is part of how segmenting works.

L8 · Verified

No decoration

Every visual element on screen is referenced by the narration. Our eval harness treats interesting-but-extraneous content as a failure, not flair.

L3 · Verified

Cues, not ornament

Arrows, colour pops and label reveals are signals that point at what the narration is saying right now. Cueing lowers cognitive load and that is what buys the learning gain.

A rule is only real if you can test it.

Every video runs three layers before it ships. Layer A runs at generation time, on the scene graph, before a cent is spent on voice or rendering. A graph that fails is regenerated with the exact failures fed back in.

Layer A

Mechanical

Plain assertions on the scene graph and word timings. Duration bands, 3 to 7 elements per scene, every element's reveal word landing within ±500ms, question-plus-recap ending. Pass or fail, no model calls.

Layer B

Semantic

A model-as-judge pass scored 1 to 5: one idea per scene, icon fidelity, whether the analogy maps to the mechanism, a seductive-detail scan. Any dimension below 3 fails the release gate.

Layer C

Output verification

Checks on the rendered file. A contact sheet confirms no overlapping elements and legible labels, a frame burst confirms the outline-then-fill reveal, audio is loudness-normalized with no clipped sentences.

API and MCP

Explainer video as an API call. Scene graph included.

One POST queues the render. Poll the record or listen to the live status stream, then receive the MP4 and its full editable scene graph.

# queue a render
curl -X POST https://sketchie.ai/api/v1/explainer \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_..." \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "input": "Explain how DNS resolves a domain name",
    "length": "0:30"
  }'

# 202 Accepted → poll GET /v1/explainer/:id
# ready → { videoUrl, sceneGraph }

# then change one scene, in words
POST /v1/explainer/{id}/edit
{ "instruction": "Make the second scene shorter and warmer" }

The scene graph is writable

The API does not just hand you a file, it hands you a document. Send a plain-language edit and seconds later you have a new video with one scene changed and everything else untouched. Versions stay in the explainer's history, so you can inspect or revert through the API.

MCP server

Any MCP-capable agent can generate and edit explainers as native tools: generate from a doc, inspect the scene graph, request an edit, re-render. Set up in Claude Desktop, Claude Code, the Claude app, or Cursor.

Built for production

Live completion status over Server-Sent Events with polling as a fallback. Deterministic rendering: the same scene graph renders the same video every time. Every video passes the automated eval layer before it leaves the pipeline.

Questions

The honest FAQ.

How do the free videos work?

Sign up and make 2 free videos, up to 1 minute each, with no card required. They carry a small watermark, and you get the full product including the scene editor. Editing does not use up one of your 2 free videos, and your first edit of a video is free.

How fast is it?

About a minute for a full video. That includes the script, the drawings, the narration, and the word-level timing between them. When you edit, a single scene re-renders in seconds.

What can I feed it?

A plain prompt, PDF, Word, PowerPoint, TXT or Markdown. Product docs, lesson notes, SOPs, PRDs, changelogs. Audio, video and article links work too.

Can I edit the video after it is generated?

Yes, scene by scene. Open the scene list, click a scene, and edit the heading, narration, labels or icon styling. Move between scenes with Previous and Next. When the fields are not enough, describe the change in plain language. Save, and Sketchie re-renders that scene only.

What voices are available?

Six curated narrators — Nora, Miles, Dean, Clara, Piper and Wren — with playable previews before you generate. Nora is the warm default. The same selected voice works across supported languages, so a course keeps one consistent narrator. Narrating in a voice you cloned runs a more expensive rail and adds its own surcharge.

How long can videos be?

Up to 6 minutes per video, in 30-second steps, or auto length. Longer material becomes a chaptered series. That is a deliberate design decision backed by engagement data, not a limitation we are working around.

Is there an API?

Yes, plus an MCP server for AI agents, a CLI and a TypeScript SDK. The POST queues your render and returns its ID. Poll the record or listen to the live SSE status stream. When it is ready, the record carries the video URL and full scene graph, and edits are available through the API too.

API access is included from Plus up, and API or MCP generations debit 2× plan minutes. In-app generation with a stock voice stays at 1×.

How does going over my plan work?

One rule: anything beyond your plan is 2×. Overage bills at 2× your plan's own per-minute rate, and it is opt-in and off by default, so you are never charged a surprise. With it off you stop at your plan ceiling and can upgrade. Unused minutes do not roll over.

How many people can be on my account?

Seats and shared workspaces are unlimited and never metered. Minutes are the only meter. Shared workspaces are on the way; today Sketchie is single-user.

Who is behind this?

A small team building from Lima, Peru. No venture funding.
